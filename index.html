Critical success factors

The PPT framework for AI transformation

People
Culture, capability & behaviour change

AI transformation lives or dies on whether people trust it, understand it, and choose to use it. Culture change is not a communications plan — it is a sustained behaviour change programme.

Process
Redesign before you automate

AI doesn't transform broken processes — it accelerates them. Process redesign must precede AI implementation. The question is: what should the process become, not what can AI automate?

Technology
Chosen last, not first

Technology is the enabler — not the driver. Chosen after people and process are understood. The right AI solution fits the human need, not the most sophisticated option available.

All three dimensions must move together. Transforming one while neglecting the others is the most common cause of partial adoption and wasted investment.

People — critical success factors

What makes the human side of AI transformation succeed

Executive championship

Visible, sustained leadership sponsorship. AI must be a strategic priority, not an IT project.

AI literacy at every level

Not technical training — contextual capability. Every role needs to understand what AI means for their work.

Fear-free change narrative

Address the ‘will AI take my job?’ question directly. Reframe AI as augmentation, not replacement.

Change champions network

Identify and embed advocates across every team to model new behaviours and create psychological safety.

Workforce planning for the AI era

Redesign roles, not just retrain people. Identify what humans will do that AI cannot.

Incentivise experimentation

Recognise and reward those who experiment and learn — not just those who succeed.

Process & technology — critical success factors

Design the process first. Choose the technology last.

Process
Process discovery before automation
Map current-state with empathy. Understand workarounds and working norms to understand the as-is.
Governance & ethics framework
Define where AI decides, where it recommends, and where humans always lead.
Data quality as a prerequisite
AI is only as good as its inputs. Data governance is a process transformation, not a technical one.
Compliance & regulatory alignment
Embed GDPR, audit trails, and essential regulatory requirements from day one — not as an afterthought.
Technology
Build-buy-partner strategy
Establish what the organisation needs to own vs. what a vendor can deliver better and faster.
AI Centre of Excellence
A central body governing standards, sharing patterns, and preventing fragmented AI sprawl.
Phased rollout over big-bang
Start with high-value, low-risk use cases to build confidence and generate visible wins.
Measure outcomes, not outputs
Define AI success in measurable goals (SMART) — decision quality, time saved, error reduction — not technical ones.

Each stage produces tangible artefacts that make the work visible, shareable, and actionable.

Stages 01 & 02 — Empathise & Define

Listen deeply. Then define the right problem.

Empathise

Sit with the people who do the work. Shadow, interview, observe. Don't ask what they want from AI — ask what drains them, where they create workarounds, what they wish someone would fix.

Activities: 1:1 empathy interviews · Observation · Contextual inquiry · Day-in-the-life exercises · Persona creation

Define

Synthesise the raw insight. Build user personas representing the real spectrum of people affected. Create current-state journey maps to reveal every friction point. Then craft point-of-view statements and "how might we" questions that reframe the AI challenge in human terms.

Activities: Affinity mapping · PoV statements · HMW framing

Stages 03 & 04 — Ideate & Prototype

Generate boldly. Build cheaply. Learn fast.

Ideate & analyse

With the HMW questions as fuel, cross-functional teams generate a wide range of ideas — some incremental, some radical. Then apply structured analysis to narrow: effort/impact mapping, feasibility scoring, AI-appropriateness assessment.

Prototype

A prototype is not a finished product — it is a learning device. Make the concept tangible enough that a real user can interact with it and react honestly.

From practice: in the EY Labs workshop I facilitated, teams produced physical prototypes in hours. The insights from users handling those prototypes shaped final solutions more than any requirements document.
Stage 05 — Test

Get real feedback from real users — and have the courage to change course.

Moderated usability testing
Facilitator watches a user complete real tasks using a think-aloud protocol.
Unmoderated testing
Users interact independently, capturing natural behaviour without the observer effect.
A/B concept testing
Two versions tested with different user groups, removing preference bias.
Co-creation workshops
Users become active designers, building ownership of the solution.
Desirability testing
Surfaces emotional and trust dimensions that functional testing misses.
Diary studies
Users document experience over days or weeks, revealing patterns over time.

Transformation philosophy

Five principles that should not be compromised

01
Empathy before architecture
You cannot design a good solution for a user you don't understand. Every transformation begins with listening.
02
Small wins build big trust
AI scepticism is earned. A visible, useful win in the first 90 days changes minds faster than any comms plan.
03
Failure at prototype is the goal
Design programmes where it's safe to test ideas and find they don't work. Learning at prototype is far cheaper than at deployment.
04
Champions, not managers
Transformation is not imposed from the top — it grows from within. Invest in people who will carry change forward after the programme ends.
05
Measure what matters to humans
Model accuracy is not a transformation metric. Time saved, decisions improved, trust increased — those tell you if it worked.
The bottom line

AI is not the transformation.
AI enables the transformation.

The transformation is what happens when people change how they work, how they decide, and how they trust — and that requires Design Thinking, not just data science.
